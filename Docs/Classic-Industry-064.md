# Classic industry: project rules P

This iteration connects industrial shipments, moving construction wagons, timed factories, resource production and civilization-specific heavy artillery. It covers the British (`coast`), Dutch (`river`), French (`france`) and Ottomans (`ottoman`). Wayfarer Cities and Highland Guild retain their original trade/manufacturing systems; equivalent late-game content for those two civilizations remains future work.

The feature is a project adaptation, not a claim to reproduce the complete current AoE III: DE technology tree or balance. The final current-source run passed 796/796 tests (466 EditMode and 330 PlayMode), and four controlled industrial saves now have actual final-client operation/visual records. See [Tests](Evidence/064/Tests/README.md) and [Native/Industry](Evidence/064/Native/Industry/README.md). These receipts bind runtime commit `5cce74ccf7222133af9bad633923f452b100bfb2`, source fingerprint `8342b85bf835fd48d5c4dfc8f612ed64770fc5b7d026d96c4f539caaa96d79b0`, and native GUID `48cbcf92c92f409c945f83eeb9b8d500`. They do not establish normal-opening industrial progression, combat balance or pressure-performance acceptance.

## Playable chain

1. Include `Factory Wagon` or `Robber Barons` in the selected deck. Both are age-IV, once-only cards and deliver one real `factory_wagon` after ordinary shipment travel. Default classic presets include both cards within the existing deck capacity. Each shipment spends a normally earned shipment opportunity; it does not create a completed factory.
2. Select the delivered wagon and place a factory in current vision. The normal footprint, terrain, bridge, unit and resource checks apply. A deployment order reserves its intended site against another friendly deploying wagon using the same conservative grid clearance as actual placement, while the site remains physically open until arrival.
3. The wagon moves through normal land navigation to an approach outside the foundation. It must use bridges across a river. If a friendly actor occupies the approach, bounded route probes select another body-safe construction side outside the future occupied cells. Placement is checked again on arrival. An occupied or invalid site waits for a retry; Stop cancels the deployment while retaining the wagon. Only a valid arrival consumes the wagon and creates an unfinished foundation.
4. The foundation takes 60 seconds before producing anything. Its construction uses the common building-speed technology factor. Ordinary workers cannot accelerate its autonomous construction. Cancellation or destruction after deployment does not restore the once-only shipment opportunity or the consumed wagon. No resource refund is invented for the wagon entitlement.
5. Select the completed factory and choose food, wood, coin or artillery. A resource mode credits only that resource to the real passive-income ledger. Artillery mode stops resource income and advances one heavy gun's production timer. Research pauses factory production while its queue remains occupied.
6. A completed gun waits for population capacity and a legal, unoccupied exit. Progress is retained while waiting, including after save/load. A successful spawn charges its actual population, uses the factory rally order, awards production XP and emits a production event. The ordinary Train command cannot bypass this mode-based production.

Living factories, unfinished foundations, living wagons and in-flight factory wagons share a limit of two per player. Two once-only cards are the normal source of those entitlements. Destroying a factory opens space but does not make an already consumed card reusable.

Mode changes discard unfinished artillery progress. Re-selecting the same mode preserves progress. Income aggregation flushes the old resource mode before changing its label, so a food bucket cannot be relabeled as wood. Factory construction progress, mode, gun progress and moving deployment orders use the existing match snapshot mechanism.

## Balance and provenance

The official [AoE III: DE release-day gameplay changelog](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/aoe3de-baseline-update/) distinguishes Heavy Cannon, Great Bombard and Rocket as units without tangible training-resource costs and records the British Rocket's 90-second production baseline and projectile speed of 45. The current [Update 19.18309](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-iii-definitive-edition-update-19-18309/) changes the Great Bombard's production time to match Heavy Cannons but does not publish the complete current unit stat table. The [Update 43871](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/aoeiiide-update-43871/) names the factory improvements Cannery, Water Power, Steam Power and Mass Production.

The civilization mapping follows the classic identity: British Rockets, Ottoman Great Bombards and French/Dutch Heavy Cannons. Factory artillery does not subtract resources per gun; choosing artillery sacrifices the factory's resource production. Exact current AoE III: DE nominal repair values and full numerical stats have not been independently verified from a primary stat table. Every numerical combat value below is explicitly project balance **P**. The 90-second Rocket baseline and its projectile speed have historical official support, rather than a claim that every current DE modifier is reproduced.

The factory occupies 4 × 4, has 4,000 base hit points, and produces 5.5 resources/second in exactly one mode. Its four paid factory improvements cost 250 food and 250 wood and take 45 seconds: Cannery adds 30% food income; Water Power adds 30% wood income; Steam Power adds 30% coin income; Mass Production adds 30% artillery production speed. Those costs, percentages and times are P. Each matching imperial artillery improvement costs 1,000 food and 1,000 coin, takes 60 seconds and adds 50% base health/attack to current and future matching guns; these are also P. No extra unofficial Robber Barons bonus is implied by that card's name.

Zero direct construction/production expenditure does not mean free repair. `repairCost` records a separate nominal value P: factory 1,000 wood; Heavy Cannon 200 wood + 500 coin; Rocket 100 wood + 500 coin; Great Bombard 100 wood + 600 coin. Repair charges the existing proportional repair rule against that value; `cost` remains zero for the wagon entitlement and factory artillery production. These nominal repair values have not been verified as exact current-DE values. The repair regression uses an ordinary worker to reach a damaged factory, verifies no healing at zero resources, then verifies real spending and healing with an explicit controlled repair budget.

## Verification boundaries

`IndustrialEconomy064Tests` exercises the public Ship → moving wagon → timed foundation → FactoryMode → actual artillery chain for all four classic civilizations, exclusive resource income, paid resource-specific research, two in-flight reservations, invalid/unseen/worker placement, Stop and reuse, blocked exits, normal paid housing release, and snapshots during shipment, movement, construction and production. Its existing resource/research case now explicitly checks frozen resource balances and passive-income counters during paid research, switches to artillery while research is still pending to check that its clock is also paused, and verifies that artillery progress and resource income both resume afterward. The cross-bank deployment test checks every real wagon step against static navigation and rejects water traversal outside bridges.

These are controlled lifecycle fixtures: age IV and earned-XP input are isolated deliberately. They do not establish that an unaided competitive AI reaches industry from its opening, and do not replace a normal-match economy acceptance sample. Numbers of catalog definitions, copied rank upgrades and named cards are not counted as independent mechanisms.

## AI industrial decisions

The AI uses ordinary shipment, wagon Build, FactoryMode and Rally commands. It orders completed factories by stable entity ID: the first supplies the largest current shortfall against its existing economy/next-age target; the second chooses its civilization's heavy artillery when at least six regular land guards are available and fewer than two matching guns are alive. Explorers, artillery, civilians and garrisoned actors do not count as those guards. Two guns are a strategy target, not a world production limit. An already committed gun finishes before changing modes if the guard force or resource balance changes; a real production event releases that commitment at the next scheduled decision. Factory research keeps its normal production pause.

Housing decisions subtract both used and queued population and use the actual factory gun's six/seven-population requirement. When those slots are missing and the player cannot afford the normal house's wood cost, the resource factory prioritizes wood rather than food or an age-saving target. Houses still require ordinary payment, a legal site and a worker. Neither the policy nor its housing choice gives resources, capacity or completed guns.

`AiIndustrialSaveTests.AiShipsTwoRealFactoriesProducesItsHeavyArtilleryAndColdRestoresTheSameDecisions` adds a bounded French fixture with controlled age IV, two explicit XP inputs, six regular guards, zero starting resource balance and an unautomated opponent. The two factories must come from real AI shipments and moving wagon deployments; production must then select artillery, set its rally and produce a real seven-population Heavy Cannon. A cold JSON restore during committed production must preserve modes, gun progress and the complete subsequent world/controller snapshots. This deliberately isolates industrial decisions; it does not verify unaided opening progression, competitive AI strength or normal-match production timing. The case is Passed in the archived final `run-LOUCjHNW` EditMode XML.

The complete church/arsenal, paid mercenary shipments, fort wagons, full current-DE unique-unit rosters, revolution replacement decks and capitol economy remain outside this industrial increment. The current DE Independence mode has separate production rules and is not used as the baseline for this normal-shipment implementation.

## Controlled industrial previews

The Editor-only entry point `Frontier.Editor.IndustrialPreviewExport.Run` exports four visual fixture saves after the final current-source EditMode and PlayMode run. Set `FRONTIER_REPORT_PATH` to a fresh empty output directory. `FRONTIER_VERIFIED_TEST_REPORTS` may identify the final test directory; otherwise the exporter uses `Builds/Tests/latest-run.txt`. It refuses stale source evidence, incomplete test reports, a nonempty destination, frozen source directories and the player's actual persistent `Saves` directory. It does not call project preparation or modify a scene.

Each civilization uses Winding's original starting entities, resources and terrain, an I–V age cap and an explicitly controlled age-IV player. AI controllers are empty. The selected deck is replaced with only `factory_wagon` and `robber_barons`; this two-card display deck is a controlled fixture, not a validated normal 25-card opening deck. Age, that deck replacement and the missing XP needed for the two industrial shipment opportunities are controlled inputs; resources, health and entities are not granted. An existing explorer walks to reveal a harbor, an existing worker pays for and constructs its dock, and each factory follows the real Ship → arriving wagon → moving deployment → timed construction chain. One factory produces coin and one produces the matching civilization artillery. The Ottoman automatic-settler system is paused through its ordinary command for this fixture.

Outputs are `industrial-coast.json`, `industrial-river.json`, `industrial-france.json` and `industrial-ottoman.json`, four corresponding case reports and `manifest.json`. Every save has a unique match ID, the current application version, a paused world, no automated players, an orthographic camera size of 15 focused on the industrial harbor, and the artillery factory selected. Reports bind the exact source fingerprint, passing-test receipt, save hash, real entity IDs, modes, positions, production events, controlled fixture inputs and actual economic ledger. Export failures retain their reports; reruns require another empty directory.

The exporter does not capture images. Its `fixtureScope` is controlled age IV, explicit XP and the two-card industrial deck with no AI controllers, while ordinary resources, paid harbor construction and the real industrial command chain remain in effect. The raw exporter files are preserved separately from the later native captures.

The corresponding `nativeScope` has now been recorded in [Native/Industry](Evidence/064/Native/Industry/README.md): ten actual F9 JSON/PNG pairs in the final client, tied to each saved match ID and build GUID. All four show coin and artillery factories; selected-unit screenshots show British ROCKET, French/Dutch HEAVY CANNON and Ottoman GREAT BOMBARD. The British native case additionally switches artillery → wood → artillery, with a measured wood balance increase. Fixture generation alone is not native rendering evidence; these later F9 files supply that evidence while retaining the controlled inputs. Neither scope is an unaided normal-opening AI match, a natural ended industrial match, or competitive balance evidence.
